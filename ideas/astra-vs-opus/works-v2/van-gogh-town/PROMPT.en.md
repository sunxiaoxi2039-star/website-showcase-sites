# The Prompt to Generate This Piece in One Shot

> You are a graphics programmer and art director. Using Three.js (r18x, ES modules + an import map, with local `vendor/three/three.module.js` and `three.core.js`, no CDN, no build tools), build a single-page web piece: **"走进梵高的小镇" (Walk Through Van Gogh's Town)**, reimagining six Van Gogh paintings as a 3D town you can walk through in first person, where everything looks like oil-paint brushwork. All UI text in Chinese.
>
> ## 1. The six paintings and the town's geography (it must be one connected town, not six exhibition rooms)
> Coordinates: x east, z south, y up, in metres.
> 1. **Café Terrace at Night** (1888): a north–south cobbled main street at the centre of town. On the west side, the café: yellow walls, a brightly lit ground floor, a huge yellow awning sloping outward and down, and under it an orange-yellow terrace with round tables and chairs, plus a wall lamp with an enormous halo. On the east side, blue-violet night-time buildings with lit windows. A steepled church at the end of the street.
> 2. **The Yellow House (The Street)** (1888): Place Lamartine to the northeast. The square has a sandy surface; the Yellow House is a two-storey corner building with green shutters and door. Next door on the left, a pink restaurant with a striped awning. On the right, a street runs north and passes under a stone railway viaduct (which continues out over the river).
> 3. **The Bedroom in Arles** (1888): inside the Yellow House, and you must be able to actually walk in through the front door. The room is trapezoidal (one wall is slanted), with pale violet-blue walls, a reddish-brown floor with green strokes, a yellow wooden bed with a scarlet coverlet and lemon-yellow pillows, two rush-seated chairs, a blue jug on the small table by the window, a blue door, and a few small pictures, a mirror and a towel on the walls.
> 4. **Starry Night Over the Rhône** (1888): the riverbank east of town. The near bank is a gently sloping lawn (with pink-violet strokes), with two or three small boats moored and a couple out for a stroll. On the far bank, a row of silhouetted buildings with lit windows; a line of gas lamps along the embankment whose light draws yellow-orange reflection columns on the water. The Big Dipper in the sky.
> 5. **Wheatfield with Crows** (1890): the wheatfield west of town. Three red-earth paths (left, centre, right) branch from a junction, with green grassy verges. The wheat is tens of thousands of golden and ochre brushstroke sprites swaying in the wind. The sky is deep blue and churning, with two elongated white clouds and twenty or thirty crows circling and flapping.
> 6. **The Starry Night** (1889): a small hill southwest of town. On the summit, a flame-shaped cypress about 17 m tall (made of thousands of dark-green, olive and brown-black strokes). From the summit, looking northeast, you overlook the whole town, the church steeple and the blue Alpilles in the distance; the sky has a huge double swirl, stars with concentric halos, and an orange crescent moon.
> Terrain: the town is flat; the wheatfield rolls gently; a ring of hills closes off the horizon, lower to the west, with a mountain range to the north. The world boundary is a circle, and the river cannot be waded. All buildings have collision. Fill the town with procedurally generated blocks of buildings (cream, ochre, salmon-pink, pale blue and pale violet walls, terracotta pitched roofs, coloured shutters, about 40% of windows lit at night), plus street lamps, trees in the square and passers-by.
>
> ## 2. Style: it must look painted, not filtered
> 1. **Per-pixel procedural brushstrokes in world space.** Write one `ShaderMaterial` for all static geometry.
>    - Do a triplanar projection by the normal's dominant axis to get planar coordinates, then dice them into a grid by "cell size," with each cell owning one stroke. Each stroke has a random offset, a length (half-length of 0.6–1.0 cells), a width (length / aspect ratio) and an angle (base angle + jitter + an optional noise flow field); the width tapers along the length, and the shape is a round-ended capsule.
>    - Use two interleaved grids and find, in a 3×3 neighbourhood, the highest-priority covering stroke (the topmost), while also recording the runner-up.
>    - **Compute lighting at the world position of the stroke's centre**, so pools of light are quantized into blocks of solid-colour strokes.
>    - Each stroke: a colour picked from a 3-colour palette by hash, with jitter; a raised highlight, bristle marks, thick at the start and thin at the end; an optional dark outline (for cobblestones). The stroke's antialiased edge blends into the runner-up stroke, not into a dark gap.
> 2. **Two-level LOD**: when a stroke cell is smaller than about 4 pixels on screen, fade to a coarse stroke layer 4× larger, and only further out fade to the average colour. Judge LOD by the geometric mean of the two directional derivatives, so ground at grazing angles doesn't flatten too early.
> 3. **Every vertex carries a palette and stroke parameters** (3 colours, cell size, aspect ratio, angle, jitter, emission, flow field, outline, size). Merge all buildings into one mesh drawn in a single call.
> 4. **Night grade**: at night, apply a hue shift (toward blue, violet and green) only to the ambient part, while the lamp-lit parts stay warm. This is "a night scene without black." Lamp falloff should be steep so the other side of the street isn't lit orange too.
> 5. **The sky**:
>    - First render a colour field and a direction field into equirectangular RTs (colour 1024×512, direction 512×256; direction stored as double-angle vectors so axial directions can blend). The colour field includes gradients, horizontal rippling bands, several swirls with spiral bands, concentric halos around stars, the crescent moon and the wheatfield clouds; the direction field is a horizontal flow plus tangential flow around the swirls, stars and moon.
>    - The sky-sphere shader divides it into rows by latitude, with the number of cells per row varying with cos(latitude), and closes the seam with a longitude modulo. Each stroke takes the colour at its centre and its angle from the direction field. Star cores and the crescent are layered on analytically, with the star cores twinkling slightly.
> 6. **Sprite strokes**: wheat, grass, cypresses, tree crowns and passers-by are all instanced cylindrical-billboard strokes — tapered capsules with bristle marks, swaying in the wind, lit per instance. Passers-by are built from a dozen or so strokes: legs, coat, arms, head, hat brim. Gas-lamp halos are segmented concentric arcs of strokes, from the inside out white, yellow, orange, yellow-green. Crows are flapping M-shaped strokes.
> 7. **Water**: also laid with strokes (long horizontal strokes, stretched 2.2× in depth). Each stroke's colour = deep blue + reflection from the sky colour field + gas-lamp reflection columns computed by "aligning the camera-to-lamp azimuth." The reflections fade as the camera rises.
> 8. **Post-processing**:
>    - Sample depth snapped to texel centres, and draw hand-drawn Prussian-blue outlines from the depth Laplacian and discontinuities. Add slight jitter to the colour sampling separately.
>    - Saturation around 1.14, a woven canvas texture with grain, a slight vignette.
>
> ## 3. "Walk into a painting, and it becomes that painting"
> Each painting has a centre and inner/outer radii (the bedroom uses the room's bounding box). Blend a set of atmosphere parameters by camera position: how much it's night, zenith colour, horizon colour, swirl strength, stars, moon, Big Dipper, clouds, turbulence, sun direction and colour, sky light, ground light, fog, lamp strength, ground colour. Interpolate smoothly over time.
> - The café, the Rhône and The Starry Night are at night; the Yellow House and the bedroom are daytime under a cobalt sky; the wheatfield is a gloomy, churning day.
> - Pass only the nearest 8 lamps into the shader at a time.
>
> ## 4. Interaction and interface
> - WASD or arrow keys to walk, Shift to walk faster, Q/E to turn. Drag the mouse to look around; click to lock the pointer. On phones, the left half of the screen is a virtual joystick and dragging on the right half looks around. Collision uses segments plus circles, with a slight walking bob.
> - Each painting has a "painter's viewpoint" (position + look-at point + FOV), with an easel standing beside it whose canvas shows that painting's palette.
>   - Number keys 1–6 or the 6 colour-swatch buttons at the bottom: fly over the rooftops to the easel. During the flight, look in the direction of travel first, and line up with the original's composition on landing. Entering or leaving the bedroom must pass through the doorway.
> - T for an auto tour (7.5 seconds per stop; any input interrupts it). F for frame mode: overlay a gold frame and nameplate at the current painting's true aspect ratio, darkening outside the frame. H hides the UI; M toggles ambient sound (synthesized with WebAudio: wind, river, crickets, café chatter, mixed by zone).
> - On entering a zone, a beige museum label appears bottom-left: the Chinese name, original title, year, place and collection, plus a line from Van Gogh's letters in Chinese translation. It hides itself after a few seconds. A round minimap sits at the top right.
> - An opening in which "the town paints itself": first a beige canvas, then strokes land one by one over about 3.4 seconds (strokes appear progressively by hash threshold, sprites and halos too). The opening UI overlays the left side of the picture: the large title 「走进梵高的小镇」, a short description, two buttons — "Start walking" and "Follow the tour" — and key help. The background camera drifts slowly.
> - At the top of the page, a thin 34 px top bar: on the left, 「← 三方对照」 linking to `../../compare/index.html`; in the middle, the work's name and a small tag 「Opus 5.5 · 独立逆向」; on the right, 「原作 @petergostev ↗」 linking to the original post. No horizontal overflow on a 390 px-wide screen.
> - Performance: reduce sprite counts when software rendering or a phone is detected; adapt the internal render resolution by frame time (0.45–1.5). Provide debug parameters `?vp=<id>`, `?t=<seconds>`, `?scale=`, `?q=`, `?frames=N` (stop after rendering N frames, for screenshots) and `?cover` (hide all UI).
>
> ## 5. Deliverables
> `index.html`, `js/*.js`, `vendor/three/`, relative paths only, statically hostable. No errors of any kind in the console.
